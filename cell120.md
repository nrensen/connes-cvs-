## 1. Part 1: Route 1B Proof Audit (Can Loewner Smoothness Settle $R_{\mathrm{spec}} = \mathcal{O}(1)$?)

### 1.1 The Operator-Norm Enclosure & Factorization of $R_{\mathrm{spec}}$
From Paper NR2 Lemma 8.28 and [`cell96.md`](file:///c:/data/github/connes-cvs-/cell96.md), the remote Stieltjes tail deviation satisfies the unconditional operator-norm enclosure:

$$\sum_{\ell > L} (\omega_{j, \ell} - 1) < \mathcal{E}_j^{\mathrm{op}}(N; L) \equiv \Delta_j(N) \frac{\|Q_{\mathrm{even}}^{(N)}\|_{\mathrm{op}}}{(E_{L+1}^{(N)} - E_j^{(N)})(E_{L+1}^{(N)} - E_{j+1}^{(N)})}.$$

We define the exact spectral factor:
$$\boxed{R_{\mathrm{spec}}(N, L) \equiv \frac{\|Q_{\mathrm{even}}^{(N)}\|_{\mathrm{op}}}{(E_{L+1}^{(N)} - E_j^{(N)})(E_{L+1}^{(N)} - E_{j+1}^{(N)})}.}$$

### 1.2 The Analytical Chain of Route 1B
Under a fixed Archimedean cutoff $T$, the Galerkin matrix is generated by the smooth symbol $\psi(t)$ on $[-T, T]$. By Loewner divided-difference smoothness (Paper NR1):
$$\|Q_{\mathrm{even}}^{(N)}\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le \sup_{t \in \mathbb{R}} |\psi'(t)| \le M(c, T) < \infty \qquad (\forall N \ge 1).$$
The numerator of $R_{\mathrm{spec}}$ is therefore **uniformly bounded in $N$**.

Now examine the denominator:
$$(E_{L+1}^{(N)} - E_j^{(N)})(E_{L+1}^{(N)} - E_{j+1}^{(N)}).$$
For fixed bound state $j$ and core size $L \ge j+2$, define the lower Ritz gap:
$$g_{j, L}(N) \equiv E_{L+1}^{(N)} - E_{j+1}^{(N)}.$$
Since $E_j^{(N)} < E_{j+1}^{(N)}$, we have $E_{L+1}^{(N)} - E_j^{(N)} > g_{j, L}(N)$, so:
$$(E_{L+1}^{(N)} - E_j^{(N)})(E_{L+1}^{(N)} - E_{j+1}^{(N)}) > g_{j, L}(N)^2.$$

If the lower Ritz gap satisfies the uniform positive separation condition:
$$\boxed{\inf_{N \ge L+1} g_{j, L}(N) \equiv g_* > 0,}$$
then the spectral factor satisfies the unconditional uniform bound:
$$\boxed{R_{\mathrm{spec}}(N, L) \le \frac{M(c, T)}{g_*^2} \equiv C_{\mathrm{spec}}(c, T, L) < \infty \qquad (\forall N \ge 1).}$$

### 1.3 The Strategic Impact of Route 1B
If $R_{\mathrm{spec}}(N, L) = \mathcal{O}(1)$ uniformly in $N$, the Gate 1 product simplifies radically:
$$\lim_{N \to \infty} \Delta_j(N) R_{\mathrm{spec}}(N, L) = 0 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \lim_{N \to \infty} \Delta_j(N) = 0.$$
**Under Route 1B, Gate 1 does NOT require exponential decay to beat a growing polynomial.**  
*Any* vanishing of the bound-state tunneling splitting $\Delta_j(N) \to 0$ rigorously closes Gate 1 at fixed $T$!

### 1.4 Critical Proof Audit on Route 1B
What prevents Route 1B from being a finished theorem right now?
1. **The Gap Separation Obstacle:**  
   By the Rayleigh–Ritz min-max theorem, eigenvalues of truncated Galerkin matrices are monotonically decreasing with dimension: $E_k^{(N+1)} \le E_k^{(N)}$. Both $E_{L+1}^{(N)}$ and $E_{j+1}^{(N)}$ decrease as $N$ increases. Proving analytically that their difference does not collapse to zero ($\inf_N g_{j, L}(N) > 0$) requires proving that $E_{L+1}^{(\infty)} > E_{j+1}^{(\infty)}$, which is equivalent to discrete bound-state isolation in the continuum limit.
2. **The Fixed-$T$ vs Joint-Limit Distinction:**  
   Route 1B operates strictly in Regime A (fixed $T$). If the final adelic positivity theorem requires the resolution scaling $T(N) > \alpha_N = \frac{2\pi N}{L}$ (Regime B), then $\|Q_{\mathrm{even}}^{(N)}\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ may grow logarithmically with $N$, requiring Route 1A.

## 2. Part 2: Discrete Barrier Mechanics for Route 1A (The Exponential Tunneling Engine)

### 2.1 The Tunneling Splitting Definition
For each bound state $j \in \{0, 1, 2, \dots, \bar{N}_{\mathrm{bound}}\}$ below the barrier top, the parity tunneling splitting is:
$$\Delta_j(N) \equiv E_{\mathrm{odd}, j}^{(N)} - E_{\mathrm{even}, j}^{(N)}.$$
Because the potential barrier is symmetric under parity $t \mapsto L - t$ (or $k \mapsto -k$), the even state is the symmetric bonding state and the odd state is the antisymmetric anti-bonding state. The energy difference $\Delta_j(N) > 0$ represents the quantum tunneling amplitude across the central barrier.

### 2.2 Discrete Potential Barrier & Turning Points
In the modal coordinate $m \in \{1, \dots, N\}$, the diagonal matrix elements $H_{mm} = \psi'(m) + \psi(m)/m$ and the nearest-neighbor hopping matrix elements $H_{m, m+1} = \frac{2(m\psi(m) - (m+1)\psi(m+1))}{m^2 - (m+1)^2}$ define an effective discrete Jacobi operator:
$$(H \phi)_m = t_m \phi_{m+1} + t_{m-1} \phi_{m-1} + V_{\mathrm{eff}}(m) \phi_m.$$

The classical turning points $m_1(E_j), m_2(E_j)$ are defined where the diagonal potential crosses the bound-state energy:
$$V_{\mathrm{eff}}(m) = E_j.$$
Inside the classically forbidden barrier region $m \in [m_1, m_2]$, $V_{\mathrm{eff}}(m) > E_j$.

### 2.3 The Discrete Agmon Action
In discrete Jacobi operators, the Agmon metric (discrete WKB barrier decay rate) is given by:
$$\kappa(m) = \operatorname{arccosh}\left( \frac{V_{\mathrm{eff}}(m) - E_j}{2 |t_m|} \right) \approx \ln\left( \frac{V_{\mathrm{eff}}(m) - E_j}{|t_m|} \right) \qquad (V_{\mathrm{eff}} - E_j \gg |t_m|).$$
The total discrete Agmon action across the barrier is:
$$\boxed{S_j(N) \equiv \sum_{m = m_1}^{m_2} \kappa(m) > 0.}$$
The discrete Agmon decay theorem implies that the wavefunction in the barrier decays as $\phi(m) \sim e^{-S_j(m)}$, and the tunneling splitting satisfies:
$$\boxed{\Delta_j(N) \le C_j \exp\left( -2 S_j(N) \right) = C_j e^{-\sigma_j N}, \qquad \sigma_j = \frac{2 S_j(N)}{N} > 0.}$$

### 2.4 Minimal Spectral Growth Compatibility
If $\Delta_j(N) \le C_j e^{-\sigma_j N}$ with $\sigma_j > 0$, then for any polynomial or sub-exponential spectral growth:
$$R_{\mathrm{spec}}(N, L) = \mathcal{O}(N^p) \quad (\forall p < \infty),$$
the Gate 1 product converges to zero with exponential speed:
$$\Delta_j(N) R_{\mathrm{spec}}(N, L) \le C_j N^p e^{-\sigma_j N} \longrightarrow 0 \qquad (N \to \infty).$$
**Route 1A completely eliminates the need for $R_{\mathrm{spec}} = \mathcal{O}(1)$.** It is robust against arbitrary polynomial growth of the upper spectrum.
